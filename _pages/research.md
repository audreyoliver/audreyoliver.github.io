## Research Overview

My research interests lie in using math to help create better outcomes, whether that's in patient care, public health, or the communities most affected by disease. Sometimes that means within-host models that incorporate pharmacodynamics to improve treatment strategies; other times it means population-level models that capture how disease spreads across space and time. Anywhere mathematical modeling can help us understand and respond to infectious diseases is where I want to be!

Currently, my work focuses on models for vector-borne diseases like dengue. I'm working to fill in gaps in sparse surveillance data, better understand how infection passes between humans and mosquitoes, and identify communities that face barriers to reaching treatment.

    <span class="project-card__status">Current</span>
    <h3 class="project-card__title">Vector-Borne Disease Transmission</h3>
    <p>Unlike diseases that spread directly from person to person, vector-borne diseases such as dengue fever involve interactions between humans and mosquito populations. I develop mathematical frameworks that better represent these transmission dynamics.</p>
    <p>This work includes data-driven methods for estimating the effective reproduction number (R<sub>t</sub>) that explicitly incorporate mosquito-human transmission, improving outbreak assessment and the evaluation of public health interventions.</p>
    <ul class="project-card__tags">
      <li>R<sub>t</sub> estimation</li>
      <li>Compartmental models</li>
      <li>Dengue</li>

  <div class="project-card">
    <span class="project-card__status">Current</span>
    <h3 class="project-card__title">Dengue Treatment Deserts in Nepal</h3>
    <p>Timely diagnosis and care are critical for reducing severe dengue outcomes, but access to treatment is uneven across regions. This project identifies dengue treatment deserts in Nepal: areas where dengue burden is high but access to healthcare facilities is limited.</p>
    <p>By combining spatial analysis of disease burden with travel times to care, including how precipitation disrupts travel, this work aims to highlight underserved communities and inform where public health resources are most needed.</p>
